How to start a book club

Six steps from “we should start a book club” to a full year on the calendar. Name ideas and a year planner are built in.

1. Find your people

Six to ten members is a good size. Fewer, and one cancellation stalls the meeting. More, and quieter people stop talking. Start with two or three friends who read, then ask each of them to bring one person. A mix of friends and friends-of-friends keeps the talk from turning into gossip.

2. Pick a format

  • How often: once a month is the most common. It gives busy people time to finish.
  • Where: rotate homes, meet at a café or library, or meet online. Rotating homes spreads out the hosting work.
  • What you read: anything goes, or pick a lane, like mysteries, classics, nonfiction, or books by local authors.
  • How long: plan on about two hours: some catch-up time, an hour of book talk, then picking the next book.

3. Name your club

A name makes the group feel real, and it gives your group chat a title. Pick a style, add your town if you like, and tap for ideas.

      4. Agree on a few rules

      Rules sound stiff, but a few shared expectations keep a club going past the third month. Agree on these at the first meeting:

      • Come even if you didn’t finish. More people stay with a club that welcomes half-readers.
      • Spoilers are fair game at the meeting. Not in the group chat before it.
      • Everyone gets a turn to talk. The host can go around the room once before open discussion.
      • Hosts take turns. The host picks the snacks, not the whole menu.
      • Book picks are fair. Each member suggests one or two, and the group votes or spins.
      • Keep books easy to get. Check that the pick is at the library or under about $20.

      5. Choose the first book

      Pick something most people can finish in a month, around 250 to 400 pages, with characters or ideas people will disagree about.         Book club FAQ

        What do you do at a book club meeting?

        Most meetings open with 15 to 20 minutes of catching up and snacks, then about an hour on the book. The host starts with an easy question, everyone shares a first reaction, and the talk goes from there. End by confirming the next book and host.

        What if people don’t finish the book?

        Let them come anyway. Agree that the meeting may spoil the ending, and keep a few questions that work for any amount read, like what they expected or which character they’d trust.

        How do you keep a book club going?

        Keep the same day each month so nobody has to check calendars, rotate who hosts, and plan a few months ahead. A schedule everyone can see makes it easy to say yes.
