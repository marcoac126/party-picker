    How to use the questions wheel

    Open this page on one phone and pass it around. Whoever holds it spins and reads the question out loud, then the group talks it through. When the talk winds down, the next person spins.

    • Start easy: turn on only “Warm-up” for the first few spins, then add the rest.
    • Reading nonfiction? Turn on “Nonfiction” and turn off “Characters” and “Story & setting”.
    • No repeats: “Remove each question after it’s asked” is on, so the wheel deals fresh questions as you go.
    • Question doesn’t fit? Just spin again. Not every question works for every book.

    What makes a good discussion question?

    The best questions don’t have a right answer. Two people who both finished the book should be able to answer differently and both make sense. That’s why most questions here ask what you think or what you’d do, not what happened.

    All 65 book club questions

    Prefer a list? Here’s every question on the wheel, grouped by type.

    Warm-up questions

    • What was your first impression after the opening chapter, and did it hold up?
    • At what point were you hooked, or did it never quite happen?
    • If you had to rate this book out of five, what would you give it, and what would bump it up a point?
    • Where and how did you read this one? Did that change how it felt?
    • Describe the book in one word. Then explain your word.
    • What did you expect from the cover and title, and how close was it?
    • Was this a book you couldn’t put down or one you had to push through? Why?
    • Would you read it again someday? What would you notice the second time?
    • Who in your life would you hand this book to, and who would you warn away?
    • If this book were a snack or a drink, what would it be?

    Characters questions

    • Which character would you trust least with a secret, and why?
    • Which character would you most want at a dinner party? Who would you seat them next to?
    • Who changed the most from beginning to end, and what changed them?
    • Which choice a character made would you have made differently?
    • Is there a character you think the others misjudged? What did they miss?
    • Did you see yourself in any character, even a little? Which one?
    • Was there a character you wanted to root for but couldn’t? What stopped you?
    • Pick a side character. How would the story look told from their point of view?
    • If you could ask one character a single question, what would you ask?
    • Did the characters feel like real people? Who felt most and least real?
    • Where do you picture the main character five years after the last page?
    • Which relationship in the book felt the most honest, and which felt the most fragile?

    Story & setting questions

    • Did the ending work for you? Would you change it?
    • What surprised you most? Did you see it coming in hindsight?
    • Which scene felt like the turning point of the whole book?
    • How much did the setting matter? Could this story happen somewhere else?
    • Were there parts that dragged or parts that flew by too fast?
    • If you were the editor, what would you cut or add?
    • What question did the book leave unanswered, and do you mind?
    • Would you want to live in this book’s world for a week? Why or why not?
    • If this became a movie, who would you cast as the leads?
    • Why do you think the author chose this title? What would you have called it?
    • Which scene would you most want to see on screen?

    Big ideas questions

    • What do you think the author most wanted readers to take away?
    • Did this book change how you think about anything, even something small?
    • What’s one thing in this book you think our group will disagree about?
    • Was there a moment where right and wrong weren’t clear? How did you decide?
    • Did anything in the book remind you of something happening in the world right now?
    • If the book were asking readers one question, what would it be?
    • Which part made you uncomfortable, and was that the point?
    • Did the book treat all sides fairly, or did it lean one way?
    • Put yourself in the main character’s situation. What would you honestly have done?
    • Is there a lesson here, or is the book just showing life as it is?

    The writing questions

    • Share a line or passage that stuck with you. Why that one?
    • How would you describe the author’s writing style to a friend?
    • How did the narrator, or the point of view, shape the story?
    • Would you read another book by this author? What would you hope for?
    • Go back to the first page. Does it hit differently now that you’ve finished?
    • Did the way the book was built, like timelines, chapters, or narrators, help or get in the way?
    • Did the conversations between characters sound real?
    • What mood did the book leave you in, and how did the writing create it?

    Nonfiction questions

    • What’s one thing you learned that you didn’t know before?
    • Did the author convince you? What evidence worked best, and what felt thin?
    • Which fact or story from the book did you end up telling someone else?
    • What would you ask the author if you had five minutes with them?
    • Whose perspective was missing from this book?
    • Is there anything you’ll do differently after reading this?
    • Does anything in the book already feel out of date, or will it hold up?
    • Did the personal stories or the facts and research stick with you more?

    Wrap-up questions

    • What should we read next if we want something like this, or nothing like it?
    • Has tonight’s discussion changed your opinion of the book at all?
    • Was this a good book club book, even if it wasn’t your favorite? Why?
    • Sum up the book in a single sentence for someone who hasn’t read it.
    • Which character or moment came up the most tonight? Why do you think that is?
    • What do you think you’ll still remember about this book a year from now?

    Is the wheel random?

    Yes. Each spin uses your browser’s built-in random number generator, so every question on the wheel has the same chance. Nothing you do here is sent anywhere.
