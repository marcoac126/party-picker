### Why people don't tap store links (ranked, guesses backed by data where noted)
1. **Wrong intent for buying.** The top queries are "random book generator/picker". Those people want an idea, not a store. Goodreads getting 5 of 6 taps fits this (found).
2. **About half the visitors can't use our store links.** About half of the book club page's visitors are outside the US (found: US 44%). Bookshop.org serves only the US and UK (found, Wikipedia).
3. **We don't know how many visitors spin.** Store links appear only in the result card after a spin (found in the page code). If only a third of visitors spin, our tap rate per spin is ~12% and fine. If everyone spins, it's 4% and weak. **We need a spin count before changing the links.** ($0, one GoatCounter event.)
4. **The result card gives no reason to act.** It shows a title and links, with no "why this book". The themed page fixes this with a one-line reason per book (testable, see experiments).

### Milestone check (10/31: ~200 book club visitors/month or a first Bookshop sale by 12/31)
- **Visitors: on pace, but propped up.** 148 visits in ~21 days ≈ 210/month. Take out Reddit (44, possibly a one-off spike) and our own visits, and the steady base is closer to **~130–150/month** (guess). Search clicks are growing (+23% in two weeks), so 200 by 12/31 is possible but not safe.
- **First Bookshop sale: unlikely by 12/31.** 1 Bookshop tap in 3 weeks. A sale usually needs many taps (guess: tens). December gift buying is our best chance, and the themed holiday page aims at exactly that.
- **Verdict:** Don't rethink yet. Re-judge on 12/31 using visits **with own visits excluded**.

### Pinterest Nov 30 rule (<~20 outbound clicks in November = drop)

- Recommendation for 11/30: **stop all new pin work.** Leave the live pins and the already-connected RSS feed alone (zero effort, zero cost). No exception for the new holiday books page. The rule exists so we don't keep making excuses for a channel that doesn't click.
- Do the planned 11/30 audience re-export only if it takes under 10 minutes. It won't change the decision.

## Part 2: Themed book browsing (occasion → mood)

### Is there search demand? (found, with limits)
- **No exact monthly volumes.** I have no free keyword tool that covers these exact phrases. Keywords Everywhere's top-883 Christmas list (global) doesn't show "christmas books for book club" in its visible 300 rows. It does show huge related demand: "a christmas carol" 246K/month and "christmas movies" 823K/month ([KWE](https://keywordseverywhere.com/ctl/top/christmas-keywords)). Treat the book phrases as long tail: real, but much smaller.
- **Strong indirect evidence:** publishers rewrite these lists every year, and the results pages are full of them:
  - "christmas books for book club": Book Riot, Romper, Bustle, Penguin Random House, Early Bird Books, bookclubs.com, bookclubchat.com (yearly 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 editions).
  - bookclubchat's "Christmas Book Club Picks for 2026" came out Oct 26, 2026 with 11 books ([bookclubchat](https://bookclubchat.com/books/christmas-book-club-picks-for-2026/)). Its December 2026 picks came out Nov 16 and say "I like to add at least one holiday read a year" ([bookclubchat Dec](https://bookclubchat.com/books/book-club-picks-for-december-2026/)).
  - "christmas romance books": Book Riot published its 2026 holiday romance list on Oct 27 and notes "there are so many new holiday romance books out every year" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/new-holiday-romance-books-2026/)). Penguin UK/AU, Marie Claire and ThriftBooks also have lists.
  - "cozy christmas mysteries": Penguin Random House (21 books, "to read and gift this holiday season"), AARP, NYPL, CrimeReads, Book Riot, Audible, and several public libraries ([PRH](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/the-read-down/holiday-cozy-mysteries)).
  - "christmas horror books": Book Riot calls it a "niche category" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/holiday-horror-books/)). CrimeReads, Fable, Powell's, Paste, LitHub and Chicago Public Library also have lists. Smaller than romance or mystery, but a clear sub-genre.
  - "winter books for book club": bookclubs.com, Early Bird ("best book club books winter 2026"), Book Riot, Stylist, Bookshop.org's own "cozy winter reading" list.
  - "spooky books for october book club": bookclubs.com, Utah State Library, Silent Book Club, BookBub, Knopf, Bookshop.org list.
  - "valentine's book club books": Pan Macmillan, PureWow, Hachette, B&N, CBC, Goodreads genre page.
- **The mood split matches how the market already sorts these books.** bookclubs.com sorts its 35 Christmas book club books into Feel-Good Romance, Romantic Comedy, Family & Historical, Thrillers & Mysteries, Classics and Humor ([bookclubs.com](https://bookclubs.com/blog/best-christmas-book-club-books)). ### Own URLs: yes, a real page per theme
- **Don't rely on `?theme=holiday` to rank.** Our parameter URLs already show up in Search Console as "Alternate page with proper canonical" (found, keywords.md 10/22). Google folds them into the main page, so a `?theme=` URL can't rank on its own. Keep `?theme=` only as a preset for share links and in-page switching.
- **Build a static page per theme.** It has the wheel preset to the theme, mood chips, and a **crawlable list of every book with our one-line reason** (the text Google can read and rank, and a reason to tap). It links to the main picker and back, and gets its own spot on /holiday-wheels/.

### Sourcing rules for book lists
1. **Inspiration only:** we may skim Goodreads lists, library displays (NYPL, Chicago PL, Decatur, Danvers) and publisher lists to find candidates. Never copy a list's order, grouping, or wording, and never copy blurbs or descriptions.
2. **Our picks, our words:** each book gets a one-line reason we write ourselves (≤15 words, spoiler-free, about why it works *for a club*), e.g. "Short enough to finish between parties; the ending splits the room."
3. **Mix sources:** no more than ~3 books per mood from any single outside list. Pull in at least 3 places per mood (libraries + publishers + our own judgment). Mark's girlfriend's suggestions count as our own picks, and they're the best source.
4. **Verify each book:** confirm author and an in-print US edition on Bookshop (ISBN), check it fits the mood honestly (no "cozy" books with graphic violence), and flag mature content as the "Frequently challenged" genre already does.
5. **Balance:** mix new and older titles, authors from different backgrounds, and at least 2 books under ~300 pages per mood (busy December).
6. **Data format:** extend books.js entries with optional `theme` and `mood` fields, plus a `why` line. Don't put themed books into the general favorites unless they fit there.
